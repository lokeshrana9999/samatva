# Sources: before-you-send

Provenance for the rules in `skills/before-you-send/` (SKILL.md and references/strip-rules.md). Kept outside the skill folder so it never loads into context. Citations follow the evidence table and sources list in `docs/architecture.md` (section 2 and the closing sources list); the state rules come from its sections 4.2, 4.4, 4.7, 5 and 7. The shared blocks (risk, boundaries, and the arousal check) are the same in every skill that carries them and are not covered here. Two sources below are not in docs/architecture.md and are marked as added.

## SKILL.md

- **No message to anyone while flooded, not even a calmer version:** docs/architecture.md section 4.4, policy filter: "no drafting of messages, posts or scripts addressed to someone else while arousal is not `calm`"; section 1 lists "drafting messages while the user is angry" as a non-goal. Gottman, The Science of Trust: nobody takes in new information while physiologically flooded (high). "Not even a calmer version" is named because a softened draft is the loophole a sycophantic model takes: Stanford, delusional-spiral transcript analysis, ACM FAccT 2026, and TrustMH-Bench, arXiv 2603.03047 (unconstrained models validate harmful plans and fail to refuse; high for the risk, low for prevalence).
- **The gate: the signals, two turns settling or calm, a claim of calm doesn't count:** docs/architecture.md section 4.2, arousal gate (length trend, absolutes, second-person accusations, capitals and punctuation, the same grievance repeated; "settling or better for two consecutive turns"; hysteresis). Section 4.5: the gate must not share a prompt with the conversational call, "so a sycophantic conversational model cannot talk its way past them"; a skill can't separate the two, so the closest stand-in is scoring the user's messages, never their say-so. The count is the shared arousal-check block (`_shared/arousal-check.md`: 0 signs calm, 1-2 settling, 3+ flooded; the stricter reading wins when script and count disagree), a design choice, not a study result. Skill-specific: a pasted draft is left out of the count, a single first message clears only when calm, and wanting it to hurt holds on its own.
- **A single calm message clears the gate:** design choice. The script holds on one turn by construction; without this rule a calm first request ("help me reply to my landlord, i'm annoyed") would always be declined.
- **Urgency and wanting it to hurt as signals; wanting it to hurt holds on its own:** design choice. docs/architecture.md section 4.2 lists imperatives; "right now" and "i want him to feel it" are the text forms of the urge this skill exists to slow down.
- **Wanting words to sting is not by itself a risk signal; harm to anyone's body, safety, or property is, the user's own included:** the shared risk block carries the first half, a design choice so the skill can do its job without every angry ask tripping it. A pasted draft is left out of the gate but read for risk, so a self-harm line inside it ("if you leave i'll end it") goes to the risk block instead of being stripped as a threat, and so does a vague threat near anything about where the person is; the risk block's "if you are unsure, treat it as a signal" decides the close calls.
- **Reflect how it landed on them first, then decline:** design choice: mirror their words for their own feelings only, never hostility toward someone else, so the wish to hurt and the charges against the other person are not reflected back. Vecchi, Van Hasselt, Romano 2005, Behavioral Change Stairway Model: listening and empathy before any influence (high, field-derived). Lieberman et al. 2007, Putting feelings into words, Psychological Science: putting the feeling into words reduces arousal (high).
- **No advice, options, or reassurance, nothing to do in the meantime, no lecture on waiting:** Miller & Rollnick, Motivational Interviewing, 3rd ed.: the righting reflex makes people dig in (high). docs/architecture.md section 5: the vent state must not advise or list options, and the intake state must not reassure; "nothing is lost by waiting" is reassurance, so the decline's only reason is "still sendable tomorrow".
- **"Still sendable tomorrow", offered tomorrow:** Gottman, The Science of Trust (flooding passes with a break, and talking resumes after it). A night rather than a short break is a design choice, and so is naming no activity for the pause (a list of things to do is options). When the user says it can't wait, the skill strips instead of declining, because "it will keep" would be false and could cost them: design choice. Kross & Grossmann, self-distancing and Solomon's paradox, supports reasoning from a distance, but their evidence is for a third-person stance, not for delay, so the delay borrows the idea without that evidence behind it.
- **The offer is conditional and about the message, never an invitation back:** the boundaries block bans "talk to me again tomorrow"; the Jed Foundation open letter to the AI and technology industry: dependency is cumulative (consensus, not trial-based). the README requires the decline to offer tomorrow, so the skill keeps the offer but ties it to the message ("if you still want to send it then") and bans "i'll be here" and "come talk to me".
- **Decline once, then strip their own text:** docs/architecture.md section 7: "A model that refuses on turn one and complies on turn eight has failed." The skill answers that by making the only thing it ever gives a flooded user their own words with the harm taken out, so persistence never buys a model-written message. Declining once rather than repeatedly is harm reduction by design, not a tested result: Miller & Rollnick (arguing for change provokes the other side) suggests a second refusal ends with the unedited draft being sent.
- **No verdict on the other person, no agreement the message is deserved, no "are you sure":** docs/architecture.md section 4.4, third-party protection and "evaluation of the user's plan, agreement with a plan"; README principle 2.
- **Calm branch, built from their words:** the README skills table (help with the message once calm) and principle 7 (restraint beats voice): the model's words never stand in for the user's in a message sent as them. Only a draft or points given for this message count, never earlier venting, so the model never picks which grievances go in, and no connective the user didn't write is added.
- **Exits: back to vent, a person to talk to, or "it's their call":** README principle 4 (sessions close and point at a human) and principle 2 (no verdict on anyone they mention).
- **Never send, never contact anyone:** docs/architecture.md section 4.7: "The agent never contacts anyone on its own."
- **Voice section:** README principle 7 (restraint beats voice). The decline keeps all three parts even below the no-profile length: if the voice and the content can't both fit, keep the content (design choice).

## references/strip-rules.md

- **Accusations and labels:** Gottman & Silver 1999, The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work (added; not in docs/architecture.md): criticism attacks the person's character where a complaint names a specific behaviour, and contempt includes name-calling and sarcasm. The skill keeps the complaint (plain facts, the request) and strips the criticism and the contempt, including criticism framed as a feeling ("i feel like you ...") or a question, and sarcasm aimed at the person (stripped as a label).
- **Absolutes:** Rosenberg 2003, Nonviolent Communication: A Language of Life, 2nd ed. (added; not in docs/architecture.md): evaluation mixed into observation, with "always", "never", and "ever" as the tell. Only that split is taken. NVC's rewrite into I-statements is not, because adding words the user didn't write puts the model's voice into a message sent as them (README principle 7). docs/architecture.md section 4.2 also counts absolutes and second-person accusations as arousal signals. The user's own decision about their own money, time, or home is kept even with a "never" in it, since it states their boundary rather than evaluating the other person: design choice.
- **Threats:** design choice. Telling people, exposing, "you'll see", and cutting someone off as payback are the forms that come up in this kind of message; a threat to anyone's body or safety, the user's own included, and a vague threat near anything about the person's whereabouts, are routed to the risk block, not stripped (docs/architecture.md section 4.3).
- **Visible markers instead of a silent rewrite:** design choice. The user sees what came out and can put any of it back themselves, so the edit stays theirs. A fixed marker format is also what lets the evals fail the baseline by construction.
- **Never repeat a removed phrase or its charge word:** design choice. The reply is what gets copied, so a removed phrase quoted back in a list of changes can still end up sent.
- **Add nothing; keep case, spelling, and language:** README principle 7: never change what a reply says to fit the voice.

## Specifics removed from the skill (as of 2026-10-23)

- Gottman's ~100 bpm flooding threshold: not used; the gate reads text only.
- Turn latency (docs/architecture.md section 4.2): a skill can't see it; dropped.
- docs/architecture.md section 4.4's regenerate-up-to-two-retries-then-stock-reflection filter: a skill has no controller to regenerate with, so the rules sit in the "before sending" checklist instead.
